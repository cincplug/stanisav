## Who is Stanisav?

Stanisav is an animated 3D character who physically visualizes the linguistic properties of languages.

## What does he do?

Every language has different properties. They can be isolating, agglutinative, fusional or polysynthetic. Some have declension, others have more genders or phonemes. They have different word order and also different level of its flexibility. Some are tonal, some have pitch accent, evidentiality, verb aspect and all kinds of properties, just like some dogs have bigger muzzle and some cats have longer tail.

These properties are visualised by Stanisav by mapping them to physical properties of his face.

- The more phonemes, the more teeth
- The more genders, the more eyebrows
- Morphology determines the shape of his face
- Evidentiality influences how big his eyes are
- Verb aspects decides on width of his eyelids
- Word order is reflected by parts of his nose.

## Where does he live?

He lives on a planet made of languages instead of countries. No ground, no oceans, no borders. Languages are distributed evenly, regardless of their speakers count. They have generative colors, independent of national flags. You can sort them and filter them by various linguistic criteria.

Click on any language: Stanisav will get its facial features, you will hear its sample, by its native speaker and also by Stanisav's designer. Stanisav will animate following the audio visualisation, and if language has more flexible word order, Stanisav will also jump around a little.

## Who made Stanisav?

I did. I designed and programmed him, I gave him voice (my own voice) and bass guitar. I did moderately use some AI in various aspects of the project, but Stanisav is not AI, he is I.

## What can you do with this app?

I exposed most of the settings, you can expand the menu and in Controls tab you can modify practically anything. If things get out of hand, good old "Reset to defaults" is at the bottom.

## What is Kavski?

Kavski is one of last additions to the app. It is a model of naming languages differently, not by nation speaking them, but following the model of former Serbo-Croatian language dialects: Shtokavian, Kajkavian, Chakavian. These names are simply obtained from how you say word "what" in these dialects. I just expanded that model to all languages, therefore English became Whatkavian, German became Waskavian, Italian became Cosakavian etc. It's a great model, I do hope it will eventually prevail. But I am not that crazy to make it default in the app, and currently it's in just a few locale's. If you want to see Kavski names, choose it from Label content dropdown in the menu.
